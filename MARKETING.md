## 1. Market Landscape

### The Opportunity

- **300,000+ churches** in the United States
- The average church has **65 members** — most run on volunteer staff or a solo pastor
- Only **~10% of churches** have a dedicated communications person
- Social media has become an expectation for congregant engagement and community outreach
- The average church spends **5–15 hours/week** on content creation — often unpaid labor from a volunteer or the pastor's spouse
- Church budgets for software are small but growing: the average church spends $200–600/mo on SaaS tools (ChurchTrac, Planning Center, Mailchimp, Canva, Buffer)

### The Pain

Churches know they need to post. They don't have time to do it well. The sermon is the only content they reliably produce every week — and they're not using it.

### The Wedge

The prototype promise — **paste a YouTube URL, get a week of content** — is a zero-friction entry point that proves the product before asking for money or a signup. This is the most important marketing asset in existence.

### Competitive Landscape

| Competitor | What They Do | Why SundayFlow Wins |
|---|---|---|
| Canva | DIY design | Still requires content creation effort |
| Buffer / Hootsuite | Scheduling | No content generation; generic |
| ChatGPT (manual) | Generic AI | No church context, no automation, no voice |
| Pro Church Tools | Strategy coaching | Human time-intensive; no automation |
| Descript | Video editing | Complex; not church-specific |
| Custom agency | Full service | $1,500–5,000/mo; out of reach for most |

**No one is doing sermon-native, voice-matched, fully automated content.** This is a category creation, not a feature fight.

## 2. Intent Tier Analysis

Understanding where a church is in their content journey determines how to reach them and what message lands.

### Tier 0 — Unaware (est. 40% of market)

**"We post occasionally. We don't really have a strategy."**

- Small rural churches, older congregations, pastor does everything
- Not actively searching for solutions
- Pain is invisible — they've normalized the problem
- **Reach via**: pastor conferences, word of mouth from peer pastors, YouTube discovery
- **Message**: "Other churches your size are doing this automatically now."
- **Goal**: Create awareness that the problem is solvable

### Tier 1 — Aware / Curious (est. 30% of market)

**"We know we need to post more. We just don't have time."**

- Actively frustrated; have tried Canva, Mailchimp, maybe hired a volunteer
- Searching YouTube: "church social media strategy", "how to create church content"
- **Reach via**: SEO content, YouTube tutorials, Facebook Groups for pastors
- **Message**: "Your sermon is already 80% of the content. We do the other 80%."
- **Goal**: Get the demo click

### Tier 2 — Actively Seeking (est. 20% of market)

**"We're evaluating tools. What actually works for churches?"**

- Googling "church content creation software", "AI for churches", "sermon to social media"
- Comparing options; reading reviews on G2, Capterra, church tech blogs
- **Reach via**: SEO landing pages, podcast appearances, ProductHunt, church tech communities
- **Message**: "Built specifically for churches. Sounds like your pastor, not a robot."
- **Goal**: Trial signup or demo booking

### Tier 3 — Ready to Buy (est. 10% of market)

**"We're spending 10+ hours/week on this. We need to fix it."**

- Churches with a part-time comms person or a pastor who is drowning
- Budget already allocated; just need the right tool
- **Reach via**: Direct outreach, Google Ads on high-intent keywords, referrals
- **Message**: ROI. Time saved. Cost vs. a part-time hire.
- **Goal**: Paid conversion immediately

### Tier 4 — Power Users / Expansion (existing customers)

**"This works. What else can it do?"**

- Churches that have used the product for 3+ months
- Want archive mining, voice cloning, multi-campus, social publishing
- **Reach via**: In-app prompts, email sequences, customer success calls
- **Message**: "Your voice profile is ready. Unlock audio devotionals in your pastor's voice."
- **Goal**: Upgrade to Growth or Pro

## 4. Positioning & Messaging

### Core Positioning Statement

> SundayFlow is the only tool that turns a Sunday sermon into a full week of content — automatically, in your pastor's voice — in under 10 minutes.

### Message Hierarchy

**Headline** (awareness): "Your sermon is already 80% of your content. We do the rest."

**Value statement** (consideration): "Upload your sermon video or paste a YouTube URL. Get social posts, video clips, a blog post, an email newsletter, a small group guide, and a 5-day devotional — all written in your pastor's voice — before lunch."

**Proof statement** (conversion): "Built for the 300,000 churches that don't have a full-time comms team. Starts at $29/month."

**Retention hook** (expansion): "After 5 sermons, SundayFlow sounds exactly like your pastor. The longer you use it, the better it gets."

### Tagline Options (test all three)

1. "Upload your sermon. Get a week of content. Ten minutes."
2. "Every sermon deserves a full week of reach."
3. "Your voice. Every platform. Every week."

### What NOT to Say

- Don't lead with "AI" — churches are skeptical of AI replacing pastoral authenticity
- Don't say "automate your ministry" — sounds dehumanizing
- Don't compete on features — compete on **outcomes and time**

## 5. Go-to-Market Phases

### Phase 0 — Demo First (Now → Month 1)

**Objective**: Prove the product with zero signup friction. Get 50 URLs processed.

**Tactics**:
- Launch the free demo: paste any YouTube sermon URL → get full content package
- Share the demo link in 5 pastor Facebook Groups with a post: "I built this for fun. Curious what y'all think."
- No pitch. No landing page. Just the URL and a screenshot of output.
- Collect email addresses of people who use it ("want to know when we launch?")

**Success criteria**: 50 demo uses, 100 email list signups, 3+ testimonials

---

### Phase 1 — Waitlist & Community (Month 1–2)

**Objective**: Build an engaged pre-launch list of 500. Create social proof before open launch.

**Tactics**:
- Launch a simple landing page: headline, 60-second demo video, waitlist form
- Post the demo video on Twitter/X, LinkedIn, Instagram, and YouTube Shorts
- Submit to ProductHunt "upcoming" and church tech newsletters (ChurchTech Today, Ministry Tech)
- Write 3 SEO blog posts targeting: "church social media strategy", "AI tools for churches", "how to repurpose sermons"
- Reach out to 10 pastors personally — not cold email. Voice notes on Instagram DM.

**Success criteria**: 500 waitlist signups, 1 paid beta customer, 1 church partner testimonial on video

---

### Phase 2 — Beta Launch (Month 2–3)

**Objective**: Convert 20 paying beta customers. Validate pricing and workflow.

**Tactics**:
- Invite top 50 waitlist signups to a beta at 50% off first 3 months
- Host a live Zoom demo every Tuesday at 12pm — record and repurpose
- Offer a "white glove" first sermon setup call for beta customers
- Ask for video testimonials after first week of content
- Track NPS weekly. Fix friction points fast.

**Success criteria**: 20 paying customers, $580 MRR, 3 video testimonials, NPS > 40

---

### Phase 3 — Open Launch (Month 3–4)

**Objective**: Public launch to convert pipeline. Target $3K MRR.

**Tactics**:
- ProductHunt launch (plan 2 weeks in advance; recruit hunters)
- Press outreach to Christian tech press: ChurchTech Today, Ministry Tech, Relevant Magazine, Christianity Today digital team
- Podcast tour: Carey Nieuwhof Leadership Podcast, Pro Church Tools, Church FX, The Unstuck Church
- Launch affiliate program: $15/mo per referral for other pastors
- Google Ads: test 5 high-intent keywords (budget: $10/day to start)

**Success criteria**: 100 customers, $3,500 MRR, 1 press mention, ProductHunt Top 5 in category

---

### Phase 4 — Growth Engine (Month 4–12)

**Objective**: Build repeatable acquisition. Target $15K MRR by month 12.

**Tactics**:
- SEO content machine: 2 posts/week on church content strategy topics
- YouTube channel: "How I built an AI tool for churches" series + tutorial content
- Partner with church planting networks (Acts 29, AMS, SBC church planting)
- Conference presence: Exponential, Orange Conference, NRB
- Launch "Church of the Month" spotlight — free month of Pro in exchange for case study
- Build referral flywheel: every customer gets a shareable link for 1 free month

**Success criteria**: 400 customers, $15K MRR, 2 conference appearances, 1 denominational partnership

## 6. Channel Strategy

### Channel Priority Matrix

| Channel | Intent | Effort | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pastor Facebook Groups | High | Low | **#1** |
| YouTube (build-in-public + tutorials) | Medium | High | **#2** |
| Twitter/X (founder brand) | Low → Medium | Low | **#3** |
| SEO Content | High (search) | High | **#4** |
| Podcast Guesting | Medium | Medium | **#5** |
| Email to Waitlist | High | Low | **#6** |
| Google Ads (intent keywords) | Very High | Medium | **#7** — start small |
| Church Conferences | High | High | **#8** — Month 4+ |
| LinkedIn | Low | Low | **#9** — David persona |
| Instagram/Reels | Low | Medium | **#10** — Sarah persona |

### Pastor Facebook Groups (Highest ROI)

The most underrated channel in church tech marketing. There are hundreds of groups with tens of thousands of pastors.

**Top groups to target**:
- "Church Planting Network" (40K+ members)
- "Small Church Big Impact" (25K+ members)
- "Pastors & Church Leaders Network" (60K+ members)
- Denomination-specific groups (SBC, UMC, nondenominational planting groups)
- "Social Media for Churches" groups

**How to post** (not promotional):
> "I've been building a tool that turns sermon recordings into a week of content automatically. Tried it on my pastor friend's sermon and it spit out social posts, a blog, a small group guide — everything. No account needed, just paste the YouTube URL. Would love honest feedback from other pastors: [link]"

**Rule**: Give 10x before asking. Answer questions. Add value. Soft-mention the tool.

### YouTube (Long Game, High Compounding Value)

Two parallel tracks:

**Track 1 — Build in Public (founder story)**
- "I'm building an AI tool for churches. Here's week 1."
- Monthly update videos: revenue, lessons, customer stories
- Authentic, imperfect. Works because pastors trust authentic voice.

**Track 2 — Tutorial/Educational**
- "How to create a week of church content from one sermon"
- "5 social media mistakes churches make (and how to fix them)"
- "How [Church Name] grew their Instagram by 300% with one change"
- SEO-optimized titles targeting Tier 1 and Tier 2 intent

### Twitter/X (Founder Brand)

Build the founder as a credible voice in church tech + indie hacking intersection. Two audiences:
1. **Pastors** — "I understand your problem"
2. **Indie hackers / founders** — "Here's what I'm building and what I'm learning"

The crossover creates viral potential. A "building a SaaS for churches" story thread can hit both communities hard.

**Content rhythm (5x/week)**:
- Mon: Insight about church content / ministry problem
- Tue: Product update or milestone
- Wed: Customer story or testimonial
- Thu: Lesson learned / mistake made
- Fri: Revenue or growth update (transparency builds trust)

## 7. Building in Public — Social Media Journey

This is not just a marketing tactic — it's a trust-building strategy specifically calibrated for the church market, where authenticity is the highest currency.

### The Narrative Arc

Building in public for SundayFlow follows a story the audience can root for:

> *A builder who deeply respects the work of pastors, frustrated that technology has ignored 300,000 churches, decides to fix it.*

This is not a "tech bro builds church app" story. It's a "servant builder" story.

### Content Pillars

**Pillar 1 — The Problem (20% of content)**
- "Pastors work 50+ hours a week. They shouldn't spend 10 of them on social media."
- Share stats, stories, empathy posts
- No product mentions — just validating the pain

**Pillar 2 — The Build (30% of content)**
- Share real output: "Here's what SundayFlow generated from a real sermon this week"
- Behind the scenes: "Here's how the AI analyzes a sermon before writing content"
- Technical posts: "Why we use Claude instead of GPT for sermon analysis"
- Mistakes and pivots: "We thought churches wanted X. They actually want Y."

**Pillar 3 — Customer Stories (25% of content)**
- "Marcus saved 8 hours this week. Here's what he did with that time."
- Video testimonials clipped into Reels/Shorts
- Screenshot testimonials from pastors (with permission)
- Church-specific metrics: "12 posts this week vs. 2 last week"

**Pillar 4 — Transparency / Journey (25% of content)**
- Monthly revenue updates
- "We hit $1K MRR this month. Here's exactly what worked."
- Subscriber/customer milestones
- Honest posts about what isn't working

### Milestone Announcement Posts

These are high-engagement "moment" posts — schedule them as public commitments:

| Milestone | Post Type |
|---|---|
| First demo URL processed | "Someone just used SundayFlow for the first time. This is what they got." |
| 100th demo URL | "100 sermons in. Here's what we've learned." |
| First paying customer | "We charged $29 today. Best $29 I've ever made." |
| $1K MRR | Thread: "How we got to $1K MRR selling to churches with $0 in ads" |
| First video testimonial | Video share across all channels |
| First conference appearance | Live content from conference + booth pics |
| $5K MRR | Deep-dive thread with lessons learned |
| $10K MRR | Press pitch + major announcement post |

### Platform-Specific Tactics

**Twitter/X**:
- 5x/week; 1 thread/week (threads get 10x more reach)
- Reply to Carey Nieuwhof, Pro Church Tools, church planting accounts — visibility in the space
- Use hashtags sparingly: #ChurchLife #ChurchCommunications #IndieHacker

**Instagram**:
- 3x/week Reels: product demos, customer testimonials, "did you know?" content about church social media
- Stories daily: polls ("does your church post every week?"), behind-the-scenes
- Target Sarah persona — visual, practical, actionable

**YouTube**:
- 1x/week minimum: 8–15 min videos
- First 10 videos planned before launch:
  1. "I built an AI tool for churches — here's why"
  2. "Watch SundayFlow turn a real sermon into a week of content (live demo)"
  3. "Why 90% of churches fail at social media (and it's not laziness)"
  4. "Month 1 building in public: what worked, what didn't"
  5. "How to repurpose your sermon into 20 pieces of content (manually)"
  6. "We got our first paying customer — here's the story"
  7. "I tested SundayFlow on 10 different sermons. Here's what I found."
  8. "The church content strategy that actually grows congregations"
  9. "Month 2 update: $X MRR, X customers, X lessons"
  10. "Why I built this for churches and not corporate clients"

**LinkedIn**:
- 2x/week: professional case studies, product updates, church tech insights
- Target David persona and denominational leaders
- Cross-post the best Twitter threads as LinkedIn articles

## 8. Pricing Psychology

### Current Tiers — Analysis

| Tier | Price | Problem | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter $29/mo | Too low for Pro positioning but right for volume | Anchor against hiring → keep |
| Growth $49/mo | $20 delta feels cheap; may anchor too low | Consider $59/mo |
| Church Pro $89/mo | Good; feels like a "serious" tool | Keep; consider $99/mo |

### Anchoring Strategy

**Always show the "What does a part-time hire cost?" comparison on the pricing page:**

> A part-time social media coordinator costs $800–1,500/month.
> SundayFlow Starter: $29/month.
> Same output. Ten minutes on Sunday.

### Free Demo as Acquisition

The YouTube URL demo is not a lead magnet — it's the product. Let it be used freely, indefinitely. The conversion trigger is:

1. "Want to save your output?" → requires email
2. "Want to process a private video or your church's recording?" → requires paid plan
3. "Want it every week automatically?" → requires subscription

This creates a natural upgrade path without pressure.

### Annual Pricing

Add annual billing at 2 months free (~16% discount). Churches budget annually — board approval cycles align with annual subscriptions. Annual billing also dramatically reduces churn.

| Tier | Monthly | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Starter | $29/mo | $290/yr ($24/mo) |
| Growth | $59/mo | $590/yr ($49/mo) |
| Church Pro | $99/mo | $990/yr ($82/mo) |
